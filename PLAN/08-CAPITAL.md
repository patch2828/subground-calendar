**The honest constraint:** every hour spent on Tier B is an hour not spent selling tickets, and Tier B may not pay until spring. Tier A is the near-term money.

## Rung 4 — Real borrowing, honestly assessed

**Read this section as written, including the parts that say no.**

| Vehicle | Realistic today? | True cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CDFI / community lender microloan** | **Best real option.** Arizona has CDFIs that lend to businesses banks decline, and they weigh character and cash flow, not just score | typically **8–16% APR** | **Worth pursuing.** Needs the EIN and a business account first. Weeks, not days. |

### The honest read on borrowing

**Debt is the wrong tool for the specific problem here.** The plan's cash need is **spiky** (a $625 deposit Friday, a $2,250 site-services bill on delivery) but the revenue is **90% pre-collectable** — presales arrive *before* the costs. A business whose revenue lands before its costs does not have a financing problem; it has a **sequencing** problem.

**The cheapest capital available is selling tickets four days earlier.** Shotgun's early payout does exactly that, at zero cost, and it has never been used.

**The one place borrowing genuinely fits** is On The Rocks — a **$78,196–$83,196** lump that the ladder explicitly does not self-fund at target. That is a real capital need with a real return case, and it deserves a real lender conversation in **Q4 2026, with an EIN and two quarters of clean books behind it** — not a rushed advance in September.

## Rung 5 — Non-debt capital

- **Revenue share with a partner** on a single show — split cost and upside instead of borrowing.
- **Co-promotion.** Another promoter shares the room cost. **Fairytale (Srija) is already a partner** and the Light House night was conceived exactly as a low-cost joint activation.
- **Venue revenue splits** instead of buyouts. A buyout is a bet; a door split is shared risk. **Worth asking Fillmore about explicitly** — the current terms are buyout-only, which is the most capital-intensive structure available.
- **Trade.** 0FD offered product instead of cash and Ryan declined because he needed money. That instinct is right when cash is the constraint — but trade is real capital when what you need is *goods*, and merch inventory is currently capped at $440.
